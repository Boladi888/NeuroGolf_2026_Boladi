# Compression-native ARC solvers and minimal exact networks for known discrete rules

## Scope and environment

[PROMPT] This note treats your competition setup as fixed: the task is **not** few-shot ARC inference, but the construction of the **smallest static ONNX** that computes a **fully known** discrete grid transformation exactly on the entire input distribution. The scoring objective is dominated by initializer bytes plus summed node-output tensor bytes under static shapes, with exactness determined after thresholding outputs at `> 0.0`. The central question is therefore: **what offline methods can help design a smaller exact computation graph for a known function, without any training or search at inference time?**

[PROMPT] Throughout, I use the following transfer test. A method is **unusable at ship time** if it needs per-instance optimization, gradient descent, program search, or iterative refinement during inference. A method is **usable offline** if it can emit a fixed artifact before deployment: for example, a minimized Boolean circuit, a threshold network, a DSL program, a topology, or a concrete weight set that can then be compiled by hand into a static ONNX.

## CompressARC and descendants

[PRIMARY] CompressARC, introduced by Liao and Gu, frames ARC solving as **minimum-description-length inference**: it uses a small neural **decoder**, but the **encoder is not another network**. Instead, encoding is carried out by **gradient descent during inference time**, which optimizes decoder parameters (and the associated compressed representation) so that the puzzle is represented as compactly as possible while still decoding correctly. The public paper abstract reports a **76K-parameter** model with **no pretraining** that solves **20% of ARC-AGI-1 evaluation puzzles** using only the target puzzle itself. ARC Prize’s 2025 materials describe the system as processing a puzzle in roughly **20 minutes on a single RTX 4070**, and public ARC Prize competition materials summarize its results as roughly **20–34% on ARC-AGI-1** and around **4% on ARC-AGI-2**. citeturn5search6turn5search1turn7search2turn5search10

[SECONDARY] The best public technical descriptions I found beyond the paper abstract characterize the decoder as a **small residual network** with **equivariance to reversible transformations** of the task, such as rotations and reflections. However, the accessible public snippets I found do **not** expose enough layer-by-layer primary detail to reconstruct the exact decoder block structure, channel schedule, or parameter allocation from text alone. What is clear is the architectural split: **a learned decoder plus an inference-time optimization loop that writes the “program” into weights**. citeturn7search20turn5search18turn5search1

[SECONDARY] By 2026, the line had been **noticed and cited**, but not turned into a fixed-graph exact-network synthesis recipe. ARC Prize’s HRM analysis explicitly argues that the important parts of HRM are the **outer refinement loop** and **task-specific memorization / adaptation**, and says this makes it conceptually similar to Liao and Gu’s test-time-training regime. The 2026 living ARC survey likewise presents CompressARC as evidence for a “compression as intelligence” story built on inference-time search and refinement, not as a source of tiny exact deployable graphs. citeturn8view0turn7search10

**Unusable at ship time.** [PRIMARY] The core of CompressARC is precisely the part your contest forbids: **per-task optimization during inference**. The encoder is gradient descent; the model starts untrained; and the solution is encoded into weights at test time. That makes CompressARC a conceptual cousin of program synthesis or test-time training, not a directly shippable static ONNX recipe. citeturn5search1turn5search6

**Usable offline outputs.** [DERIVED] There is only one transfer path that looks plausible for your setting: **symmetry-aware decoder design**. If a known rule is equivariant under a small transformation group, then offline you could emit a **fixed tied-weight subgraph** or a **hard-coded augment/de-augment voting scaffold**. The output artifact would be a symmetry-reduced circuit template, not an optimizer. But this is only a design hint, not a proven compression win: a **76K-parameter** test-time-trained residual model lives in a very different size regime from your **100–25,000-byte** hand-built exact graphs. citeturn7search20turn5search6

## MDL and program-synthesis-as-compression for ARC

[PRIMARY] The strongest “compression-like” ARC work before CompressARC is the DreamCoder family. The 2024 paper *Neural networks for abstraction and reasoning* adapts **DreamCoder** to ARC, introduces the **PeARL** DSL, and adds a recognition model for guiding search; its repository exposes solved tasks together with the synthesized programs. A parallel practical lineage comes from **Michael Hodel’s ARC-DSL**, whose repository presents a compact DSL plus proof-of-concept solver programs for ARC tasks. In both cases, the emitted artifact is an **exact symbolic program**, not a neural computation graph. citeturn15search1turn16search1turn12view5

[PRIMARY] Bober-Irizar and Banerjee’s DreamCoder-on-ARC result is important because it is one of the clearest ARC instances of **library learning / abstraction learning**: the system writes programs in a pure functional DSL and can reuse learned abstractions. Public summaries of the same work report that the DreamCoder implementation achieved **16.5% on ARC-Easy and 4.5% on ARC-Hard**, which is modest in score terms but highly relevant for your question because the output artifact is already a **program**, not a floating-point predictor. Hodel’s ARC-DSL is likewise directly interesting because it is intended to be expressive enough to solve arbitrary ARC tasks while staying generic, and the repository includes explicit solver code. citeturn15search1turn16search6turn12view5

[PRIMARY] Several 2024–2026 follow-ups also emit programs, but their minimality story is weaker than their search story. Ouellette’s 2024 **GridCoder / Learning the Program Space** work enumerates programs in decreasing order of **joint sequence probability** under a transformer-guided DSL model. **ConceptSearch** uses Hodel’s ARC-DSL, keeps a program database, and asks an LLM to generate programs conditioned on nearby programs and scores. **ILPAR** emits sequences of logic programs over a small object-centric DSL. **SOAR** goes further by evolving and refining **Python** programs with an LLM in a self-improving loop. All of these can emit exact executable artifacts if they succeed, but they do **not** optimize final ONNX byte count as their primary search objective. citeturn20view4turn20view6turn12view2turn12view3turn19view1

[PRIMARY] The one paper that most directly matches your “minimality machinery matters” criterion is **MADIL**. Ferré’s 2025 system is explicit about **MDL**: it represents a program as an **input decomposition model** plus an **output composition model**, defines syntax-size and description-length costs, orders descriptions by increasing DL, and uses MDL to prefer compressive decompositions. Its output artifact is not an imperative tensor graph but a **descriptive task model**. Still, this is the clearest blueprint for a size-first offline synthesis loop: replace MADIL’s syntax-length cost with **your exact ONNX byte cost**, and search over decompositions / compiled subgraphs instead of over generic symbolic descriptions. citeturn19view0turn21view0turn21view1turn21view6

**Usable offline outputs.** [DERIVED] The useful outputs here are: a **DSL program** (DreamCoder, Hodel, GridCoder, ConceptSearch, SOAR), a **learned library of abstractions** (DreamCoder-like systems), or an **MDL-ranked descriptive model** (MADIL). These are all potentially useful as **offline design artifacts** that a human can compile to ONNX. None of the surveyed ARC systems emits a **minimal tensor/dataflow graph** directly. citeturn16search1turn12view5turn19view0

## Minimal exact networks for known discrete functions

[DERIVED] This is the sharpest transfer zone for your setting, and the center of gravity is **not** ARC-specific ML. It is **logic synthesis, threshold-logic mapping, and exact subcircuit optimization**. Those fields already optimize equivalent exact computations under size-like costs, and that is much closer to your problem than MDL ARC solvers or neural test-time training. citeturn27view0turn27view1turn39search13turn25search8

**Threshold-circuit synthesis.** [PRIMARY] Theory says threshold circuits are powerful but still suffer worst-case blow-up. Public complexity summaries state that every Boolean function on \(n\) variables has a **depth-two threshold circuit** with at most about **\(2^n / n\)** gates, while lower-bound work shows that a **random** Boolean function requires at least about **\(\Omega(2^n / n^2)\)** gates in depth two. In other words: threshold gates are expressive, but the worst case remains exponential, so asymptotic results are only weakly informative for your concrete 9–25 input predicates. citeturn26search13turn26search4

[PRIMARY] The practical tool story is much better for small cuts. Neutzling et al.’s ABC-based threshold mapping flow explicitly **precomputes 9-input cuts**, filters them to keep only threshold logic functions, and maps the resulting design using those threshold cuts. Their later TCAD flow reports that the method is implemented in **ABC**, can improve area/depth relative to prior threshold mappers, and is able to handle **large-fanin TLGs**, though the concrete public cut-enumeration flow remains centered on 9-input cuts and notes that earlier methods were often limited to **6 inputs**. A 2024 threshold-identification paper snippet further indicates that the practical identification frontier had only just moved from all **8-input** NP-class threshold functions to **9-input** NP classes, which is a strong signal that public exact threshold tooling is still not a commodity solution for arbitrary **25-input** predicates. **Verdict:** actionable for **9-input** windows; for **25-input** windows, expect decomposition or custom synthesis, not a mature off-the-shelf threshold oracle. The offline artifact here is a **threshold-gate netlist / threshold-mapped circuit**. citeturn27view0turn27view1turn24search19

**Weight-agnostic networks.** [PRIMARY] Gaier and Ha’s **Weight Agnostic Neural Networks** search over **topology**, not trained weights, and evaluate candidate architectures by populating them with a **single shared random weight**. The paper demonstrates minimal architectures that already perform several RL tasks and gives above-chance MNIST behavior under random/shared weights. That is intellectually adjacent to “minimal exact networks,” but the optimization target is **expected task performance without weight training**, not exact truth-table or full-distribution correctness. **Verdict:** not practically useful for your goal. The offline artifact would be a **topology**, but neither exactness nor ONNX byte cost is the optimized objective. citeturn31search1turn31search3

**Lottery-ticket and extreme pruning with exact preservation.** [PRIMARY] The pruning literature is mostly approximate, but there is a separate verification-based thread on **exact preservation**. Gokulanathan et al. 2020 use verification queries to find neurons that never affect outputs and report reductions of up to **10%** while remaining **completely equivalent** to the original network. Lahav and Katz 2021 generalize this into pruning and slicing that can guarantee equivalence either exactly or up to a prescribed tolerance. Ladner and Althoff 2025 describe a **sound** automatic reduction that shrinks networks to a fraction of their original neurons while preserving verifiability guarantees. That is real exact-preserving compression, but it preserves equivalence to an **already existing network**, not directly to the ground-truth discrete function you care about. **Verdict:** useful as a cleanup pass only if you first build a larger correct network; not a primary route to 100–25,000-byte exact solutions. The offline artifact is a **smaller network with a proof obligation discharged against the original one**. citeturn35view1turn37view0turn35view3

**Logic-synthesis crossover.** [PRIMARY] This is the part that most clearly transfers. The exact-synthesis ecosystem already optimizes **equivalent** Boolean networks under size-like objectives. The **mockturtle** library provides AIG, MIG, and k-LUT networks plus generic synthesis algorithms. EPFL/DATE work on **Practical Exact Synthesis** and **Exact DAG-Aware Rewriting** uses SAT-based exact synthesis to replace subcircuits with smaller exact ones. Reichl et al. 2024 explicitly formulate exact subcircuit replacement as finding a **smaller equivalent** implementation for a fixed cut while preserving the overall function of the surrounding circuit. **Verdict:** this is the strongest usable offline input to your hand-compilation flow. The offline artifact is a **minimized SOP/AIG/MIG / exact cut replacement** schedule. citeturn25search8turn39search16turn39search3turn39search13

**Mandatory mapping sketch.** [DERIVED] I did **not** find prior art that directly maps ESPRESSO/ABC/mockturtle outputs into tiny static **ONNX** graphs under your exact byte-count objective, so the mapping below is my own cost sketch.

For a Boolean plane represented as a float tensor of shape `[1,1,30,30]`, one full intermediate plane contains **900 FP32 values = 3,600 bytes**. If the spatial extent is cropped or only part of the grid is materialized, the plane cost falls proportionally; your own examples of **900–3,600 bytes** per plane are therefore the right order of magnitude. Under that model:

- **NOT gate**
  - Best case: **free as an edge complement** if absorbed into the downstream threshold gate by changing the sign of the weight and shifting the bias.
  - If materialized: one scalar constant plus `Sub` or `Neg`, roughly **4 bytes of params** and **one output plane** (typically up to **3,600 bytes**).

- **AIG 2-input AND node**
  - One **1×1 Conv** (or equivalent `MatMul`) over 2 input planes with weights `[1, 1]` and bias `-1.5`, followed by the contest’s output thresholding convention.
  - Cost: **2 weights + 1 bias = 3 FP32 scalars ≈ 12 bytes params**, plus **one output plane ≈ 3,600 bytes**.
  - So a materialized 2-input AND is about **3,612 bytes** total under your metric, overwhelmingly memory-dominated.

- **2-input OR node**
  - Same pattern with weights `[1, 1]`, bias `-0.5`.
  - Cost is again about **12 bytes params + one output plane**.

- **General k-input threshold gate**
  - One 1×1 Conv from **k** input planes to 1 output plane with arbitrary signed weights and one bias.
  - Params: **\(4k + 4\)** bytes in FP32.
  - Output: **one plane**, typically **900–3,600 bytes** depending on spatial extent.
  - Example: a **25-input threshold gate** costs only about **104 bytes in params**, so in your regime **intermediate memory dominates parameter count**.

- **AIG chain versus threshold gate**
  - A raw AIG implementation of a conjunction will spend one output plane **per internal AND**.
  - A minimized **SOP / threshold** representation can often compute the same predicate with **far fewer materialized planes**, which is why Boolean minimization and threshold mapping matter more here than shaving scalar weights.

The key design consequence is simple: in your cost model, **one avoided full-plane intermediate is worth roughly hundreds of scalars**. That makes **logic sharing, complement absorption, and term factoring** more valuable than almost any param-count micro-optimization.

## Overall verdict

[DERIVED] **The single most actionable item is to add an offline logic-synthesis pass to your current hand-built flow:** export each known Boolean subproblem to **SOP/AIG/MIG**, run **ESPRESSO / ABC / mockturtle / exact cut replacement** to minimize it under a cost that reflects your ONNX byte model, and then hand-compile the minimized circuit back into **1×1 Conv / threshold / gather-scatter** primitives while aggressively absorbing complements and eliminating intermediate planes. **Nothing else I reviewed beats current hand construction** for **100–25,000-byte exact networks**: CompressARC is too inference-optimized, DreamCoder/MADIL emit symbolic artifacts rather than tensor graphs, WANs optimize the wrong objective, and exact pruning is mostly a cleanup tool after the fact. citeturn25search8turn39search13turn39search16turn27view0turn27view1turn19view0turn31search1turn37view0
