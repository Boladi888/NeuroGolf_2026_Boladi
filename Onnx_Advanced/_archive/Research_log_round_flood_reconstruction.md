## Source classes and notation

[PROMPT] I use five source-class tags throughout: **[PROMPT]** for facts given in your brief, **[PRIMARY]** for peer-reviewed papers or primary-book chapters, **[SECONDARY]** for expository notes or documentation, **[DERIVED]** for deductions from the cited sources plus your stated cost model, and **[SPECULATIVE]** for constructions I did not find proved in the literature.

[PROMPT] Let `M` be the binary mask, `S` the binary seed, and let one local reconstruction step be `reach ← dilate(reach) ∧ M`. Under your cost model, if one plane has `P = H·W·c` bytes with `c ∈ {1,2}` for U8/F16, then one unrolled local round costs about `2P` plane-bytes because it materializes one dilation result and one masked result. For the `N=30` example, one plane has `900c` bytes, so one local round costs `1800` bytes in U8 or `3600` bytes in F16.

[PRIMARY] Vincent defines binary reconstruction as the union of the connected components of the mask that intersect the marker, and also defines geodesic dilation of size `n` inside a mask as the set of mask pixels whose **geodesic** distance to the marker is at most `n`. Heijmans later rephrases geodesic dilation as dilation by balls of the **intrinsic** mask metric and states that this geodesic-dilation family satisfies semigroup relations.

## Semigroup failure under masking

[PRIMARY] The standard exact identity is this: **elementary** geodesic dilation equals “one ordinary unit dilation followed by masking.” Vincent writes that the elementary step is `(Y ⊕ B) ∩ X`, and then immediately warns that “this last statement is absolutely wrong when non-elementary geodesic dilations are considered”; Robinson later uses the same distinction, reserving **conditional dilation** for one large ordinary dilation followed by masking and **geodesic dilation of size N** for `N` iterated elementary constrained steps.

[PRIMARY] At the same time, the **abstract** geodesic-dilation family defined in the intrinsic mask metric does satisfy semigroup relations in the morphology literature. In other words, if `δ_M^r` means the true radius-`r` geodesic dilation in the mask metric, then the family behaves like a metric-ball semigroup.

[DERIVED] The exact answer to your first question is therefore **two-part**. If “doubling structuring elements” means **true intrinsic geodesic balls** of radii `1,2,4,8,…` inside the mask metric, then semigroup logic is fine. If it means the implementable operator “use a larger Euclidean/grid footprint, then mask once,” masking breaks the shortcut and the schedule is **not exact in general**. The obstruction is that ambient grid distance can be small while mask-geodesic distance is much larger around bottlenecks, folds, and concavities. Vincent’s own Figure 5 is an example of ambient closeness with larger geodesic distance inside the mask.

[DERIVED] A concrete counterexample is a 1-pixel-wide **U-corridor** on the 8-neighbor grid: let the mask be
`M = {(0,0),(0,1),(0,2),(1,2),(2,2),(2,1),(2,0)}`
and the seed be `S = {(0,0)}`. The target `(2,0)` lies within ambient Chebyshev distance `2`, so one **conditional** radius-2 square dilation followed by masking includes it. But inside `M`, every valid 8-neighbor path from `(0,0)` to `(2,0)` has length `4`, so it is **not** in the 2-step geodesic dilation. This is exactly the “narrow concave corridor” failure mode you flagged. The same reasoning gives an even simpler disconnected-set counterexample, but the U-corridor shows that the failure persists even when the mask component is connected. This deduction uses Vincent’s geodesic-distance definition and his warning that non-elementary geodesic dilation is not obtained by one large conditional dilation.

[PRIMARY] The morphology literature’s exact acceleration path reflects this. Vincent’s “parallel reconstruction” is the naive full-image iterate-to-stability scheme; he says it is easy on parallel machines but may require “sometimes several hundreds” of complete scans. He then develops sequential, FIFO-queue, and fast-hybrid exact algorithms instead of power-of-two conditional dilations. Robinson’s downhill filter is another exact alternative that avoids repeated blind scans by ordered propagation, and Teodoro et al. report that their irregular-wavefront GPU implementation of morphological reconstruction is exact with respect to the sequential counterpart.

## Provably correct sublinear-round schemes

[DERIVED] Because final binary reconstruction is exactly “take the seed-containing connected component(s) of the mask,” any **general** exact `o(K)`-round method must either compute connectivity or shortest-path information by a more global primitive, or else assume mask structure strong enough to certify large local hops. There is no evidence in the primary morphology literature I found for a generally exact `O(log K)` schedule built only from larger ordinary max-pool footprints plus one mask per stage.

## Grid-specific lower bounds and local convexity

[DERIVED] For **4-connectivity on an `N×N` grid with arbitrary masks**, the minimum number of universally exact masked-dilation rounds is the **geodesic diameter from the seed set**, and that lower bound is tight. The reason is adversarially simple: if a one-round local operator can activate a pixel at any offset that is not a single 4-neighbor edge, then the mask consisting only of the seed pixel and that target pixel is already a counterexample, because the operator would create reachability without any valid mask-contained path. Therefore, among purely local masked-dilation primitives, the only universally exact elementary step is the ordinary 4-neighbor step, so a snake-shaped 1-pixel corridor of length `D` forces at least `D` rounds. This deduction is the graph-theoretic restatement of Vincent’s connected-component and geodesic-distance definitions.

[PRIMARY] Faster exact algorithms do exist once nonlocal primitives are allowed. Hsu and Lin give `O(log N)`-time image-component labeling on CRCW PRAM for an `N`-pixel image, and Liu–Tarjan–Zhong give `O(log d + log log_{m/n} n)` randomized PRAM connectivity for component diameter `d`, alongside the observation that repeated adjacency squaring already gives exact `O(log d)` time at very poor work cost.

[DERIVED] The right way to formalize “when a larger local kernel is exact” is to make the kernel match the **intrinsic metric ball** of the chosen connectivity. For 4-connectivity, the natural radius-`r` footprint is the Manhattan diamond
`D_r = {(u,v) : |u| + |v| ≤ r}`.
Define one masked diamond step by `Δ_r^M(A) = (A ⊕ D_r) ∩ M`. Then a sufficient and essentially tight local condition is:

```text
r-local Manhattan convexity relative to the current front A:
for every a ∈ A and every q ∈ M ∩ (a + D_r),
there exists a 4-neighbor path a = v0, v1, …, vt = q
with t = |qx-ax| + |qy-ay| and all vi ∈ M ∩ (a + D_r).
```

[DERIVED] Under that condition, one radius-`r` masked diamond dilation is exactly the same as `r` elementary 4-neighbor masked dilations, because every candidate point in the diamond has a shortest in-mask path of the right length that stays inside the same local window, and the converse inclusion is automatic since 4-neighbor geodesic balls are exactly Manhattan diamonds on the unobstructed grid. The condition is a local digital analogue of orthogonal or Manhattan convexity.

[DERIVED] By contrast, a **square** `k×k` max-pool is not metric-matched to 4-connectivity even on the empty grid. A `5×5` square includes corner offsets `(±2,±2)`, which are four 4-neighbor steps away, but it excludes offsets like `(3,1)` that are also four 4-neighbor steps away. So on a 4-neighbor problem, a square max-pool cannot serve as a clean fixed-radius geodesic ball. Composite square-kernel rounds can still help on structured masks, but only after you impose a stronger local condition that makes the particular offsets you expose correspond to real in-mask shortest paths. For theory, the diamond footprint is the exact object; for your current hardware primitive, square pools are natural only for the 8-neighbor case.

[DERIVED] The analogous project-relevant 8-neighbor condition is cleaner. If `S_r = {(u,v): max(|u|,|v|) ≤ r}` is the radius-`r` square, then one masked square dilation is exact for `r` elementary 8-neighbor rounds whenever, for every front pixel `a` and every candidate `q ∈ M ∩ (a+S_r)`, there is an 8-neighbor path of length `‖q-a‖∞` that stays inside `M ∩ (a+S_r)`. I use this “scale-`r` frontwise geodesic convexity” in the schedule recommendations below.

## Recommended exact schedules

[PROMPT] Your deployed primitive is a `3×3` dilation on one plane with a mask after each round, and your observed worst-case depths include `5`, `14`, and `25`, with one task maxing at `11`. Under your cost model, all local pool sizes cost about the same **per round**, so the best exact local schedule is always “use the largest kernel whose larger hop you can certify as geodesically valid.”

[DERIVED] For the project-like **8-neighbor** interpretation of `3×3` square dilation, `5×5` is the natural radius-2 square. If the mask is certified to be scale-2 frontwise geodesically convex at every `5×5` round, then `5×5` compresses two exact `3×3` rounds into one. For `D=11`, that cuts local rounds from `11` to `6`, and cuts plane-bytes by the same factor. An atrous/rate-2 `3×3` schedule has the same nominal round count but a stricter visibility condition, so in this cost model it is dominated by plain `5×5`. All row costs below use `2 × rounds × 30² × c` with `c=1` for U8 and `c=2` for F16. The exactness logic follows from Vincent’s intrinsic geodesic definition plus the scale-2 local-convexity criterion above.

[DERIVED] For your reported corpus depths, the best local exact schedule therefore has a simple form. With **no structural certificate**, the optimum is still the baseline `3×3 × D`. With a verified **scale-2 frontwise geodesic-convexity** certificate, the optimum becomes `5×5 × ⌈D/2⌉`, giving exact round counts `3`, `7`, and `13` for depths `5`, `14`, and `25`, respectively. That is the cleanest exact speedup available inside the current pool-and-mask primitive family.

[DERIVED] The overall bottom line is blunt: **there is no generally exact “O(log K) by doubling ordinary max-pool footprints” shortcut for masked reconstruction.** Exact `O(log D)` or `O(log N)` algorithms exist, but they are connectivity or matrix-closure algorithms with nonlocal state. If you stay inside unrolled single-plane MaxPool-plus-mask rounds, the only provable exact acceleration comes from validating enough local mask geometry that larger hops become true geodesic hops. Among the options you named, `5×5` is the only local schedule I would recommend pursuing first.
